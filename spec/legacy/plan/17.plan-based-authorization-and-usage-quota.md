# 17. Plan-Based Authorization and Usage Quotas

> Give every account a plan (`free` / `basic` / `pro` / `max` / `master`) that grants a monthly credit balance,
> held at job start and settled against the ledger, plus the master-only publishing + admin surfaces | Scope: FS |
> Status: built (job 37), re-denominated in credits by change 19 (job 47)

## Purpose

Postpilot needs test accounts that outsiders can safely use, and today it cannot have them: every authenticated
account has identical, unlimited authority. Any session may start unbounded LLM work on the most expensive models
(Claude Opus 5 at $5/$25 per million tokens), pair a publishing agent, and drive the operator's Naver publishing
pipeline. The only authorization that exists is "has a session".

This plan introduces **plan-based authorization**. As built by job 37 it bounded an account along three USD axes
(daily starts, daily budget, monthly budget) plus a per-model plan floor. **Change 19 replaced all four with one
axis**: each account carries one plan — `free`, `basic`, `pro`, `max`, or `master` — and the plan grants a monthly
balance of credits, where a credit is a product-fixed $0.01 of list value.

A tier now decides exactly two things:

1. **The monthly credit grant** — 50 / 200 / 500 / 1000, and unlimited for `master`.
2. **The master-only surfaces** — the automated Naver publishing path and account administration.

Which models an account may run is decided by what its balance can afford, not by its tier.

`master` is the operator tier: unlimited on all three axes, and the only plan that can use the automated Naver
publishing surface (publishing runs through our paired agent and our infrastructure — [I1]) or administer other
accounts' plans.

The tier structure is deliberately shaped like a future subscription ladder (PRD §9 leaves billing out of scope,
but the quota ledger, the plan column, and the admission gate are exactly the primitives a paid plan needs later).
This plan builds the enforcement machinery; it does not build payments.

## Scope / Non-goals

### In scope

- A `plan` attribute on every account: `free` / `basic` / `pro` / `max` / `master`, stored in SQLite, defaulting to
  `free` for new accounts; migration 0013 backfilled existing accounts to `master` so current operator use did not
  regress, and 0019 widened the ladder.
- A **usage ledger**: every server-side LLM call persists its token counts and resolved cost per account (today
  this usage is computed and then discarded outside the A/B experiment path).
- A **credit gate** applied when any LLM-consuming job is started (generation, revision, voice analysis/learning,
  profile validation, write A/B comparison): the worst case the work could cost is held against the account's lots
  up front, and refused with a machine-readable `INSUFFICIENT_CREDITS` error when the balance cannot cover it. The
  hold is settled against the ledger when the job ends, and the remainder returned.
- **Affordability, not a floor**: every account may select and run every registry model. `ListModels` tells the
  frontend what each model would cost the caller and whether the balance covers it, so pickers can disable and
  label — display only, never the gate.
- **Master-only publishing**: the entire human-facing `PublishingService` surface (pairing, agent management,
  StartPublish, retry/cancel) requires `master`; the frontend hides publishing UI from other plans.
- **Plan administration**: `adduser --plan`, a `setplan` CLI, and master-only RPCs (`ListUsers`, `SetUserPlan`)
  with a minimal master-only `/admin` screen, so plans change without a redeploy.
- **Self-visibility**: a `GetMyPlan` RPC returning the account's plan, its credit balance and the lots behind it,
  the renewal instant, and every rung on offer — surfaced in the plan badge and in a `/plans` comparison screen so
  a user can see why an action was refused and when it unblocks.

### Non-goals

- Payments, checkout, subscription lifecycle, invoices, or any billing integration — the plan ladder is shaped for
  it, but no money moves.
- Self-service signup or plan upgrades; accounts remain operator-provisioned (plan 01), and only `master` changes
  plans.
- Per-model or per-stage rate limiting, concurrency limits, or request throttling beyond the three axes above.
- Charging for non-LLM resources (storage, uploads, publish jobs). The paired agent's deterministic local publisher
  performs no LLM inference and is not metered.
- Hard mid-flight cancellation when a balance runs out: enforcement is at the hold, so a job that costs more than
  its estimate is absorbed by us rather than by the account, and never killed mid-flight ([I5]).
- Retroactive usage reconstruction for work done before this plan lands; the ledger starts empty.
- Organization/team plans; a plan binds to a single account.

## Design

### The plan ladder

Plans are an ordered ladder `free < basic < max < master`. All numeric limits and the ladder itself are
code-owned product rules (a single Go table, surfaced to the FE via `GetMyPlan` — the FE never hardcodes a limit):

| Plan     | Daily LLM-job starts | Daily budget | Monthly budget | Models                   | Publishing | Admin |
| -------- | -------------------- | ------------ | -------------- | ------------------------ | ---------- | ----- |
| `free`   | 10                   | $0.10        | $2.00          | `min_plan: free` only    | —          | —     |
| `basic`  | 30                   | $0.50        | $12.00         | `min_plan: free · basic` | —          | —     |
| `max`    | 100                  | $1.00        | $25.00         | all registry models      | —          | —     |
| `master` | unlimited            | unlimited    | unlimited      | all registry models      | ✅         | ✅    |

The free monthly budget ($2.00) is a derived safety cap (the user-specified caps are free $0.10/day, basic
$0.50/day + $12/month, max $1.00/day + $25/month); every plan carries all three numeric limits so the schema stays
uniform. Budgets are stored and compared in **micro-USD** (`int64`), matching the existing `cost_microusd`
convention.

Daily and monthly windows are **calendar day / calendar month in `Asia/Seoul`** — a fixed product constant, not
env config. Reset instants are computed from that zone and returned to the client.

### Data model (one migration, next free number)

- `users` gains `plan TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'free' CHECK (plan IN ('free','basic','max','master'))`. The migration
  backfills all pre-existing rows to `'master'` (they are the operator's accounts; nothing regresses).
- `usage_admissions(id INTEGER PK, user_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE, kind TEXT NOT
  NULL, job_id TEXT NOT NULL, created_at TEXT NOT NULL)` — one row per **admitted** LLM-job start, written in the
  same transaction that creates the job. The daily-count axis counts these rows, so refused admissions and
  non-LLM work never consume the count. Index `(user_id, created_at)`.
- `usage_events(id INTEGER PK, user_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE, kind TEXT NOT NULL,
  job_id TEXT NOT NULL, stage TEXT NOT NULL, model TEXT NOT NULL, prompt_tokens INTEGER NOT NULL,
  completion_tokens INTEGER NOT NULL, cost_microusd INTEGER NOT NULL, cost_source TEXT NOT NULL CHECK
  (cost_source IN ('reported','estimated','unavailable')), created_at TEXT NOT NULL)` — one row per completed
  server-side LLM call (including failed calls whose usage the provider reported — job 23 already preserves
  those). The budget axes sum `cost_microusd` over the window. Index `(user_id, created_at)`.
- Ledger rows are kept indefinitely (they are billing groundwork); compaction is a future concern.
- `model_experiment_candidates` keeps its own usage columns for leaderboard analytics; experiment calls **also**
  write `usage_events` rows so the account ledger remains the single quota source.

### Backend placement (ARCHITECTURE.md §2)

- **`backend/internal/plan`** (new leaf domain package): the `Plan` type, the ordered ladder, `ParsePlan`, the
  limits table, and `Allows(min Plan) bool`. No dependencies; importable by `auth`, `llm`, `usage`, and RPC
  handlers.
- **`backend/internal/usage`** (new context): owns both tables; exposes
  - `Admit(ctx, Start) error` — checks the model floors and then the three numeric axes, and records the admission,
    all inside **one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction** so two concurrent starts cannot both pass the same window;
    returns a typed quota error naming the exhausted axis, the limit, current usage, and the reset instant;
  - `Release(ctx, jobID)` — drops an admission whose job row was then not created, on a context that outlives the
    caller's cancellation. It is the only row this context deletes;
  - `Record` / `RecordCall(ctx, ref, usage, failed)` — persists one LLM call's usage, reusing the reported →
    estimated → unavailable cost resolution (which now lives in `internal/llm`, the package that owns the prices, so
    `experiment` and `usage` share one resolver);
  - `Summary(ctx, userID) (Summary, error)` — today's start count, today's and this month's cost, reset instants.
- **`backend/internal/auth`**: `users.plan` in the domain `User`, queries, and session context — the acting
  user's plan rides the request context beside the user id. The RPC interceptor gains a **master-only procedure
  set** (all nine human `PublishingService` procedures + the admin RPCs) enforced closed-by-default, exactly like
  the existing `publicProcedures` / `agentProcedures` sets. `GetMe` (login/session probe) now returns the plan.
- **`backend/internal/llm`**: `modelEntry`/`ModelInfo` gain `min_plan` (required on every model in
  `config/providers.yaml`; an unknown value fails boot, same posture as any other bad registry entry). The
  registry stays ignorant of users; it only carries the declared floor.
- **`backend/internal/provider`**: `ListModels` computes `locked` per model for the calling plan;
  `SaveSelection` / `SaveComparisonPair` / `ApplyRecommendationSet` refuse refs whose `min_plan` exceeds the
  caller's plan. `GetSelections` treats a plan-locked saved selection as **locked, not deleted** (`Missing: true`
  semantics for the client, row preserved) so an upgrade restores it — unlike vanished/unsuitable rows, which are
  still deleted.
- **The enqueue seam** (`internal/job`): admission is applied **once**, at `job.Queue.Enqueue`, through a
  consumer-declared `Admitter` port wired in `cmd/api`. All seven LLM kinds are therefore gated in one place, and one
  A/B comparison is one admission even though it fans out to two candidates ([I3]'s explicit fan-out). The refs a job
  will run travel with it — including a write comparison's observe model and the stored refs an experiment or
  profile-validation **retry** re-runs, so a downgrade is not escapable by retrying yesterday's work.
- **Ledger writes happen at the llm boundary, not at each call site.** **As built:** rather than editing the twelve
  places `llm.Usage` surfaces, `cmd/api` wraps the registry every context is given, and the worker stamps the job's
  identity (`usage.Work`) on the handler's context. Every provider call is therefore recorded by construction, and a
  new call site is metered the day it is written. The trade is precision in the `stage` column: it is the job kind,
  except for a generate job's observation call, and not even then when both stages ran the same model. Master
  accounts skip the numeric checks but still write admissions and events (visibility is free).
- **Admin surface** (`auth` context + `rpc`): master-only `ListUsers` (id, plan, created_at) and
  `SetUserPlan(user_id, plan)`. A **last-master guard** refuses demoting the only remaining master (RPC and CLI
  both). **As built:** the guard is a condition inside the UPDATE statement, not a count taken before it — two
  concurrent demotions would each see two masters and both commit. CLI: `api adduser <id> [--plan=<plan>]`
  (default `free`, flag accepted in either argument order) and `api setplan <user-id> <plan>`.

### Contracts (proto)

- New `proto/postpilot/v1/plan.proto`:
  - `enum Plan { PLAN_UNSPECIFIED; PLAN_FREE; PLAN_BASIC; PLAN_MAX; PLAN_MASTER; }`
  - `PlanService.GetMyPlan(GetMyPlanRequest) → GetMyPlanResponse{ Plan plan, PlanLimits limits, PlanUsage usage }`
    where `PlanLimits{ int32 daily_job_starts, int64 daily_budget_microusd, int64 monthly_budget_microusd }`
    (0 = unlimited) and `PlanUsage{ int32 jobs_started_today, int64 cost_today_microusd, int64
    cost_month_microusd, string day_resets_at, string month_resets_at }` (RFC3339).
  - `AdminService.ListUsers` / `AdminService.SetUserPlan` (master-only via the interceptor set).
- `provider.proto`: `ModelInfo` gains `Plan min_plan` and `bool locked` (locked = the calling account's plan is
  below `min_plan`).
- `auth.proto`: the session/`GetMe` response gains `Plan plan`. **As built:** `LoginResponse` carries it too — the
  client seeds its session cache from the login answer, and a seed without the tier would render the shell's
  master-only surfaces wrongly until the next `GetMe`.
- **Error contract**: quota refusals are `resource_exhausted`; model-gate and role-gate refusals are
  `permission_denied`. Each refusal carries a machine-readable connect error detail — `reason ∈ DAILY_COUNT |
  DAILY_COST | MONTHLY_COST | MODEL_LOCKED | MASTER_ONLY`, plus `limit`, `used`, `resets_at`, and (for
  `MODEL_LOCKED`) `model`, `models`, and `required_plan` — so the FE renders exact copy instead of parsing strings.
  **As built:** the reasons take the product's existing UPPER_SNAKE spelling rather than the lower-case form drafted
  here — `frontend/src/shared/api/app-failure.ts` is one allowlist and a second casing convention in it would be a
  wart. Copy lives in plan 13's `errors` namespace in both locales, and the detail's machine values (micro-USD, an
  RFC3339 instant, the stored tier name) are rendered through i18next formatters in the browser.

### Frontend (ARCHITECTURE.md §3, FSD)

- **`entities/plan`**: `useMyPlan` query (+ mappers) over `GetMyPlan`; plan/usage formatting helpers
  (micro-USD → `$0.07`).
- **`entities/model-catalog`**: mappers pass through `min_plan` / `locked`; the shared stage-model pickers render
  locked models disabled with the required plan label (they remain visible as upsell, per the existing
  disabled-model pattern).
- **App shell account area**: a compact plan badge and usage meters (today's starts, today's and this month's
  budget) fed by `useMyPlan`, refreshed when opened. **As built (reshaped by change 11 / job 38):** the panel lives
  in `widgets/account-menu` — the header's avatar popover, which also holds the account id, the master-only `/admin`
  link, and logout — and still mounts only while the popover is open, so "refreshed when opened" falls out of the
  query rather than out of a timer. The meters use the `shared/ui/meter` primitive and its two semantic tokens; an
  unlimited axis states so instead of drawing an empty bar. (The earlier standalone `widgets/plan-usage` badge
  popover dissolved into that account popover.)
- **Quota errors**: features that start LLM work (`generate-post`, revision, voice analysis, A/B) map the typed
  refusal details to inline copy naming the exhausted axis and reset time.
- **Publishing UI**: the publish panel, agent management, and retry surfaces render only for `master`, and both
  `/publishing-agents` and `/admin` redirect a lower tier back to the app (server remains authoritative; the FE gate
  is UX).
- **`pages/admin`** (+ `features/manage-users`): master-only `/admin` route — user list with a plan selector per
  row over `ListUsers` / `SetUserPlan`; non-master navigation never links it and direct entry is refused by the
  server.

### providers.yaml model floors (initial assignment)

- `min_plan: free` — `openrouter/free`, `deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash-0731`, `z-ai/glm-5.3-flash`,
  `qwen/qwen3.8-flash`.
- `min_plan: basic` — `openai/gpt-5.6-luna`, `google/gemini-3.7-flash`, `deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro-0813`,
  `z-ai/glm-5.3`.
- `min_plan: max` — `x-ai/grok-4.6`, `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5`, `openai/gpt-5.6-sol`,
  `anthropic/claude-opus-5`.

The shipped recommendation set (`balanced-2026-08`) references `min_plan: basic/max` models; `ApplyRecommendationSet`
refuses (with `model_locked` details) when any selection in the set is locked for the caller, and the FE disables
the apply action with the required plan.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. Every account has a plan in `{free, basic, max, master}`; the migration backfills pre-existing accounts to
   `master`; `adduser` defaults to `free` and accepts `--plan`; `setplan` changes an existing account.
2. When an account at its daily start count (free 10 / basic 30 / max 100) starts any LLM-consuming job
   (generation, revision, voice analysis/learning, profile validation, A/B comparison), the server refuses with
   `resource_exhausted` and a `DAILY_COUNT` detail carrying limit, used, and the next Asia/Seoul midnight; the
   job record is not created.
3. When an account's ledger cost for the current Asia/Seoul day ≥ its daily budget (free $0.10 / basic $0.50 /
   max $1.00), or for the current month ≥ its monthly budget (free $2 / basic $12 / max $25), new LLM-job
   admissions are refused with the matching `DAILY_COST` / `MONTHLY_COST` detail; in-flight jobs are not killed
   (bounded overshoot).
4. Every server-side LLM call (success or failure with reported usage) writes one `usage_events` row with token
   counts, `cost_microusd`, and `cost_source` resolved reported → estimated → unavailable; A/B candidate calls
   appear in both the experiment table and the ledger; one A/B comparison consumes exactly one daily-count
   admission.
5. `master` accounts are never refused on any numeric axis, yet their admissions and usage events are still
   recorded.
6. A request that references a model whose `min_plan` exceeds the caller's plan — starting a job, saving a
   selection or comparison pair, or applying a recommendation set — is refused `permission_denied` with a
   `MODEL_LOCKED` detail naming the ref and required plan; `ListModels` marks such models `locked` for the
   caller, and the FE pickers show them disabled with the required plan.
7. A saved model selection that becomes plan-locked (downgrade) is reported to the client as missing/locked but
   its row is preserved, and it works again after an upgrade with no re-selection.
8. Every human `PublishingService` procedure refuses non-master callers with `permission_denied` (`MASTER_ONLY`),
   so a non-master account can neither pair an agent nor start/retry/cancel a publish; the FE shows no publishing
   UI to non-master plans.
9. `GetMyPlan` returns the caller's plan, all three limits (0 = unlimited), live usage, and both reset instants;
   the FE surfaces plan + usage in the app shell and shows axis-specific copy with the reset time when an action
   is refused.
10. `ListUsers` / `SetUserPlan` and `/admin` work for `master` and are refused `permission_denied` for every other
    plan; demoting the last remaining master is refused (RPC and CLI).
11. `providers.yaml` requires a valid `min_plan` on every model; an unknown or missing value fails boot.
12. `GetMe` (session probe) includes the plan, and the FE role-gates publishing/admin surfaces from it without an
    extra round-trip.

## Policy / Config Impact

Rules that are now true (owned by [policy/plans.md](../policy/plans.md), plus touches to
[policy/auth.md](../policy/auth.md), [policy/providers.md](../policy/providers.md),
[policy/publishing.md](../policy/publishing.md), [policy/jobs.md](../policy/jobs.md)):

- Every account carries exactly one plan; `free` is the provisioning default; only `master` (RPC/CLI) changes it;
  the last master cannot be demoted.
- LLM-job admission is gated by plan: daily starts 10/30/100/∞, daily budget $0.10/$0.50/$1.00/∞, monthly budget
  $2/$12/$25/∞ (micro-USD internally), calendar windows in Asia/Seoul.
- Enforcement is at admission; one in-flight job may overshoot a budget by its own cost.
- Every server-side LLM call is written to the account usage ledger with reported-first cost resolution.
- Model access floors live in `config/providers.yaml` (`min_plan` per model, required); the server enforces them
  on every ref-accepting RPC, the client only displays them.
- The human publishing surface and account administration are master-only.

Config values introduced (all landed):

- **Plan limits table** (starts/day, budgets) — BE code-owned product-rule table in `backend/internal/plan`
  (constants, not env); surfaced to the FE via `GetMyPlan`, never hardcoded in FE.
- **Quota timezone** `Asia/Seoul` — BE unexported constant (product rule).
- **`min_plan` per model** — deployment-editable in `config/providers.yaml`, validated at boot.
- **Migration backfill plan** (`master` for pre-existing accounts) — one-time migration constant.
- No new env vars; no FE config values (the FE reads everything from RPC responses).
