# 28. Change: Price credit holds from the stage budget of each call

> A **change proposal** (a WHAT delta) that modifies the already-shipped behavior of [plan/17.plan-based-authorization-and-usage-quota](../plan/17.plan-based-authorization-and-usage-quota.md).
> Filled by /create-change through a user interview — a reviewable doc, not a one-line prompt.
> The STEPS live in the job that /create-change-job creates. Write it in English.

## As-is

Change 19 made the balance a two-phase hold and settle, and the guarantee it bought still holds:
[`spend`](../../backend/internal/usage/service.go#L100) refuses before writing anything when the lots do not cover
the required amount, and [`spendUpTo`](../../backend/internal/usage/service.go#L269) — settlement, which "never
refuses" — takes only `min(lot.Remaining, left)` from each lot. **A balance cannot go negative.** What an
under-priced hold costs is not the account; it is us, silently, at settlement.

### The hold prices from a constant the spend side stopped using

[`worstCaseMicrousd`](../../backend/internal/usage/service.go#L181) prices **every** planned call at
`holdInputTokens` (30,000) prompt × `s.maxCompletionTokens` completion, and `maxCompletionTokens` is one number for
the whole process — `cfg.LLMMaxTokensDefault`, 8,192, handed to `usage.NewService` at
[main.go:193](../../backend/cmd/api/main.go#L193).

Change 24 had already moved the *spend* side off that constant. `LLMCompletionBudget` gives observation a budget
derived from the batch size, and writing and revision a budget derived from the requested character count, floored
at the fallback and capped at `Ceiling = fallback × writeBudgetCeilingFactor`. So the two halves disagree in both
directions today:

- An observation call is **held at 8,192** while it is **sent** the batch-derived `Observe` budget. The hold is too
  large, and a user with a thin balance is refused work that would have been cheap.
- A write for a long target is **held at 8,192** while it is **sent** up to the ceiling, a multiple of that. The
  hold is too small, and the difference is absorbed at settlement by whatever the lots happen to still hold.

The prompt side is not the problem: `holdInputTokens` is deliberately above the largest real prompt, and its
comment says why. The completion side is the half that stopped tracking reality.

### One ref used twice is one entry

[`job.PlannedCalls`](../../backend/internal/job/job.go#L123) collapses a ref chosen for two stages into a single
entry with `Count = 2`, because a stated count is how many times that *model* runs across the whole job. That is
correct while every call is priced identically. It stops being correct the moment the price depends on which stage
the call belongs to.

### Change 29 widens the second error deliberately

[Change 29](29.give-the-write-budget-reasoning-headroom.md) adds reasoning headroom to the write budget, on
purpose, to stop a reasoning model spending the whole budget thinking. Because the budget and the hold are wired
separately, that change on its own would raise what a write call is *sent* without raising what it is *held* at.
This change is what makes it honest.

## To-be

### A planned call carries the completion budget it will actually be sent

`PlannedCall` gains the completion-token figure for that call, alongside `Ref` and `Count`, and
`worstCaseMicrousd` prices each entry at its own figure instead of one service-wide constant. The admission seam
already knows the job's shape — kind, photo count, requested target length — so it can ask the same
`LLMCompletionBudget` the stage will ask.

A ref used for two stages with different budgets becomes **two entries**, one per budget. The collapse rule stays
for entries that agree, so the double-pricing it exists to prevent is unchanged.

### The quoted price and the held price follow one rule

`CreditsFor` — the number behind the catalog's per-model `RequiredCredits` and
[`EstimatePostCredits`](../../backend/internal/provider/service.go#L55) — prices with the same per-call budgets, so
what a surface quotes and what admission holds can never be derived by two different rules. Where a surface prices a
model without knowing a stage (the catalog's per-model figure), it names the stage it is quoting for rather than
falling back to a constant.

### What stays

`holdInputTokens` stays 30,000 and stays deliberately high, for the reason its comment gives. The service keeps a
completion fallback for a call that declares no budget, so a new caller cannot accidentally price a call at zero.

## Scope / Non-goals (regression boundary)

**In scope**

- `PlannedCall`, `worstCaseMicrousd` and `CreditsFor` in `internal/usage`.
- The construction sites: `job.PlannedCalls`, the `jobAdmission` bridge at the composition root, and
  `internal/provider`'s catalog pricing and post estimate.
- Whatever seam is needed for those sites to read the per-stage budget without a context reaching into another's
  configuration (ARCHITECTURE §2.2).

**Non-goals — these must NOT change**

- **The floor guarantee.** `spend` refuses before writing; `spendUpTo` clamps at each lot's remaining; a balance
  never goes negative. This is change 19's promise and this change does not touch it.
- `holdInputTokens`, and the prompt side of the estimate generally.
- Settlement: the refund path, the `actual > held` overrun path, idempotence on the open-hold predicate, and the
  `len(debits) > 0` guard that keeps an exempt tier exempt.
- The credit ladder, the charge formula (`BASE + cost × MULTIPLIER`), lot expiry and consumption order, renewal, the
  30-photo ceiling, and the plan comparison screen.
- `LLM_MAX_TOKENS_DEFAULT` itself, and every budget the stages are sent. This change re-prices the hold to match the
  budgets that already exist; it does not change a single provider request.
- The ledger's own rows (`usage_events`) and the reasoning-spend aggregate the curation surface reads.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. A generation job's hold prices its observation call at the observation budget and its write call at the write
   budget for that job's requested target length, rather than both at `LLM_MAX_TOKENS_DEFAULT`.
2. Holding for a job whose observation and write use the **same** model produces two priced entries when the two
   stages have different budgets, and the model is not charged for more calls than it makes.
3. A job with a long requested target holds strictly more than the same job with no target, and the difference
   tracks the write budget's own derivation.
4. An observation-only job (every observation reused ⇒ no observe call) still holds nothing for it — change 24's
   zero-count rule is unaffected.
5. `CreditsFor` and the admission hold return the same figure for the same planned work.
6. Balance floor regression: a user whose lots cannot cover the hold is refused before any row is written, and a
   job whose actual cost exceeds its hold drains the lots to zero and no further.
7. Settlement refunds `held − actual` to the originating lots, and a terminal transition that runs twice settles
   once.
8. No provider request body changes as a result of this change.
