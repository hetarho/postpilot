# T251 the browser composites and encodes the video track
> st:todo | ssot:CLIP-153 CLIP-12 CLIP-13 CLIP-98 CLIP-125 | base:CLIP@36 | dep:T250

## goal
The browser draws the plan's cuts, transforms and typeset text onto the output canvas and encodes them as an H.264 video track meeting CDS-52's V12.

## acceptance
- [ ] every cut is drawn over its source range at its fixed rate, with its crop, at the ratio's canvas size and 30 fps, in plan order
- [ ] a fade transition renders as a fade and a hard cut as none, matching what the plan declares
- [ ] every caption, intro line, outro line and disclosure badge is drawn from the server-typeset PNG at the position and interval its manifest states, never redrawn as browser text
- [ ] the encoded track is H.264 High at the ratio's resolution and 30 fps, and its frame count matches the edited output timeline within one frame
- [ ] vitest: a two-cut plan with a rate change, a fade and a caption produces the expected frame count, the caption is drawn only inside its interval, and the encoder is configured from the ratio and CDS-52's V12
- [ ] verify: `pnpm --filter ./frontend test` · `pnpm lint` · `pnpm lint:fsd` · `pnpm lint:style:probe && pnpm lint:style` · `pnpm build:web`

## impl notes
- the typeset text is ALREADY server-rendered: `entities/clip-project/model/preview-assets.ts` caches the PNGs the draft preview draws, with their placement manifest. The browser render reuses exactly those, so CLIP-12's "exact typeset text through approved templates" and CLIP-13's bundled fonts hold with no browser typesetting at all
- source frames: an `HTMLVideoElement` seeked per output frame and drawn with `drawImage` onto an `OffscreenCanvas`, rather than a raw `VideoDecoder` — the originals are ordinary MP4 files the element already decodes, and the element handles container parsing the decoder would need a demuxer for
- encode with `VideoEncoder` fed `VideoFrame`s built from the canvas, `avc` `High`, the ratio's canvas, 30 fps; the config comes from `CLIP_DESIGN.ratios` and CDS-52's V12, never a literal
- the work runs in a Worker so the page stays interactive; T254 owns its progress and cancellation, so expose the render as an async iterator of completed frames rather than wiring UI here
- CLIP-125 binds this: a browser render that moved a frame or a millisecond against the same plan would be a product change, so the timeline arithmetic is the plan's own resolved output intervals, not a re-derivation
