## impl notes
- the transition belongs to the cut it leads into so a reorder in step ② keeps each cut's own entry and the first cut is always a hard cut; the FE reducer already recomputes the duration on every edit and only the formula changes
- two-pass `loudnorm` is the FFmpeg-documented way to hit an integrated target on a short file; single-pass drifts with dynamic mode. The measurement pass runs on the assembled intermediate before the final encode so the final render stays one encode
- `fade_ms` stays on `ClipEditingState` for one release as a compatibility field (200) and is removed by a follow-up when no client reads it; the FE stops reading it in this task
- CDS-36's fade-through-black is accepted by validation so a manual plan may choose it later, but no control offers it now (off by default)

Two readings of the SSOT that want an update-ssot, both recorded here rather than decided quietly:

1. **CDS-35's 60 ms cross-fade cannot be an `acrossfade` at a hard cut.** `acrossfade` CONSUMES its overlap: 60 ms of audio the picture does not give back, so every cut after the first hard boundary would run 60 ms early, and 300 ms early by the fifth. The audio therefore takes exactly as much of the timeline as the picture does at each boundary — `acrossfade=ns=<transition>` where a transition overlaps, and at a hard cut a matched `afade` pair (60 ms out, 60 ms in) either side of the seam before the `concat`. That is a real 60 ms cross-fade at every boundary, and it costs the timeline nothing. The goldens prove audio and video end on the same sample in all four shapes.
2. **CDS-37 makes a single-take clip impossible, and caps the cut count.** 6.0 s a cut against CLIP-19's 15 s floor means a clip has at least three cuts, and 90 s at least fifteen; 1.2 s a cut means 90 s can hold at most 75. The recorded live response in `TestRecordedLiveClipResponses` is exactly one 15 s take and is now refused — the evidence is kept as recorded and the refusal is what it proves. `plan_cut_length` is the new refusal for a cut with no room to reach 1.2 s in either direction.

Also worth knowing:
- **A queued job is unaffected** (no payload version change), but a plan STORED before this deploy is read back with a fade at every boundary — that is what its stored duration was computed from. `EncodeEditPlan` writes version 2; versions 0 and 1 are backfilled on read.
- The transition rides the cut it leads INTO, so a reorder carries it along. Whichever cut ends up first leads in from nothing: `ApplyCorrection` and the FE reducer both normalise it rather than refusing the reorder.
- `ClipEditingState.fade_ms` stays on the wire for one release and nothing computes a duration from it any more; a follow-up may remove it.
- The loudness report is the first thing in this codebase read from FFmpeg's stderr (`Command.CaptureStderr`). It arrives at the END of the log, so a truncated stderr is a missing report and fails rather than being parsed short.
- `plan_cut_fade` now measures a cut against the transitions that actually eat into it, and keeps a 2 × 200 ms floor besides.
