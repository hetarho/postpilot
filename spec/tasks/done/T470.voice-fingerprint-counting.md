# T470 Count the fingerprint and compare a text against it

## goal
`internal/voice` gains a pure, standard-library fingerprint: the eight counted items of VOICE-24 measured over a voice's 학습 글, over a plain text and over a post's blocks, each with its example sentence and `unknown` state, plus the comparison VOICE-62 draws from two fingerprints — the foundation T471–T474 build on, with no storage, RPC or UI yet.

## acceptance
- [v] `voice/fingerprint.go` exposes `FingerprintOf(materials []Material) Fingerprint`, `MeasureText(text string) Fingerprint`, `MeasureBlocks(blocks []Block) Fingerprint` and `Compare(voice, text Fingerprint) []ItemComparison` with no I/O, clock or model call
- [v] every item follows impl notes' rule and threshold, and an item below its threshold is `Unknown` rather than 0; table tests pin each item on hand-written Korean fixtures (one-sentence-per-line posts, emoji-heavy answers, `ㅎㅎ`/`ㅋㅋ`/`ㅠㅠ` runs, `!!` repeats, headings with emoji and question marks, `-` and `①` lists, 저/나/우리 forms, the adverb lexicon with particles)
- [v] a pasted Naver post fixture yields the same fingerprint with and without its place card, 영업시간 lines, hashtags and `[출처]` line (VOICE-61 applied through T469's prose filter)
- [v] every counted item carries one example sentence with its material id, chosen deterministically (newest 학습 글 first); `MeasureText` and `MeasureBlocks` examples carry no material id
- [v] `Compare` returns one `ItemComparison` per counted item — the voice's and the text's facets in the item's own unit, the headline facet, and a distance in 0…1 — ordered by distance, largest first, with unknown items last; tests pin the order for a text that matches every habit, one that drops the exclamation marks, and one too short to show most items
- [v] the analysis prose segmentation reuses `SegmentSentences` and `endingOf`; the package still imports nothing outside the standard library for this (VOICE-51)
- [v] verify: `cd backend && test -z "$(gofmt -l .)" && go vet ./... && go build ./... && go test ./internal/voice/...`

## impl notes
- Inputs: `Material{ID string; Kind post|answer; Part opening|description|closing (answers); CreatedAt; Text string}`; `Block{Type TEXT|HEADING|LIST|QUOTE|IMAGE|VIDEO; Content string; Items []string}`; materials are processed newest first by `CreatedAt, ID` for examples and ties
- Sentences: T469's prose lines, split by `SegmentSentences`; blocks: TEXT and QUOTE contents are prose paragraphs, HEADING contents are headings, LIST items are list lines, IMAGE and VIDEO (captions included) are skipped
- ① endings — the `endingOf` mix of 다 · 해요 · 습니다 · 기타 as shares; frequent sentence-final strings: strip trailing whitespace, punctuation, emoji and compatibility jamo, then count the last 2, 3 and 4 Hangul syllables of each sentence; keep candidates with count ≥ 3 and share ≥ 0.05, drop a shorter suffix when a longer one containing it has ≥ 0.8 of its count, and keep the top 5 by count (longer first on ties, then code-point order), shown as `~더라구요`; unknown below 10 sentences
- ② sentence-final marks — after stripping trailing whitespace, emoji and jamo runs, the last mark rune classifies the sentence: `!` exclaim, `?` question, `~` or `～` tilde, `…` or two or more `.` ellipsis, one `.` period, anything else none; repeat share = sentences whose final run holds two or more of the same `!`, `?` or `~`; unknown below 10 sentences
- ③ emoji and jamo — per 100 sentences: emoji clusters (a maximal run of runes in U+1F000–U+1FAFF, U+2600–U+27BF, U+2B00–U+2BFF, U+203C, U+2049, joined by U+200D and followed by U+FE0F or U+1F3FB–U+1F3FF, counts once), runs of two or more ㅎ, of ㅋ, and of ㅠ or ㅜ; unknown below 10 sentences
- ④ shape — average sentence length in runes without trailing punctuation and spaces (today's `Measure` rule, one decimal); paragraphs split on blank lines (a TEXT block is one paragraph) with their average, minimum and maximum sentence counts; line-break share = non-empty prose lines ÷ sentences, capped at 1, "every sentence on its own line" at ≥ 0.8; unknown below 5 sentences
- ⑤ opening and closing — a post contributes its first and last prose line, an opening answer its first sentence and a closing answer its last; lines are trimmed and cut at 60 runes, deduplicated after collapsing whitespace, ordered by count then newest, three of each; a text contributes its first and last line; unknown when none exists
- ⑥ adverbs and phrases — a code-owned lexicon: 진짜 정말 너무 완전 되게 엄청 무척 아주 매우 약간 살짝 조금 좀 꽤 제법 은근 참 딱 막 그냥 확실히 특히 역시 일단 드디어 벌써 이미 다시 또 사실 솔직히 개인적으로 무엇보다 근데 그런데 그래서 그리고 그러니까 아무튼 어쨌든 게다가; a whitespace token stripped of punctuation and quotes matches a word alone or followed by one of 로 · 도 · 는 · 은 · 요; per 100 sentences, words with at least 2 hits, top 6 (lexicon order on ties); "none" is a real value from 30 sentences up, unknown below 10
- ⑦ first person — exact stripped tokens: 저 저는 저도 저를 저의 저한테 저에게 제가 제 → 저; 저희 저희는 저희도 저희가 저희의 우리 우리는 우리도 우리가 우리의 → 우리; 나 나는 나도 나를 나의 나한테 나에게 내가 내 → 나; per 100 sentences each, the dominant form when the total reaches 1 per 100, otherwise none; unknown below 10 sentences
- ⑧ headings and lists — in plain text a heading is a prose line alone in its paragraph, at most 30 runes, whose `endingOf` class is 기타 or which ends in `?`; a list line starts with `-` `•` `·` `*` `▶` `▷` `✔` `✅` `☑` `✓`, a number followed by `.` or `)`, or ①–⑳; report the heading count with its emoji, question and numbered shares, and the list-line share with the most common marker (a LIST block's items count as `-`); unknown when there is neither a heading nor a list line
- Examples: ① the first sentence of the dominant class ending in the top suffix when one exists; ② the first sentence with the most common mark other than period (period when that is all there is); ③ the first sentence holding an emoji or a jamo run; ④ the sentence closest to the average length; ⑤ the top opening line; ⑥ the first sentence holding the top word; ⑦ the first sentence holding the dominant form; ⑧ the first heading, else the first list line
- Comparison facets and distance, each in 0…1:
  | item | facets | distance |
  |---|---|---|
  | ① | the four shares, the top suffixes | half the L1 distance of the shares |
  | ② | the six mark shares, the repeat share | half the L1 distance of the mark shares |
  | ③ | emoji, ㅎㅎ, ㅋㅋ, ㅠㅠ per 100 | the largest `|a−b| / max(a, b, 1)` |
  | ④ | average length, paragraph average, line-break share | the mean of `|a−b| / max(a, 1)` for length and the line-break share gap |
  | ⑤ | openings and closings | 0.5 for an opening the text does not share (same first three syllables after normalising) plus 0.5 for a closing |
  | ⑥ | the voice's words and the text's rate of each | the share of the voice's words the text never uses |
  | ⑦ | the three rates, the dominant form | 1 when the dominant forms differ, otherwise the relative gap of the dominant rate |
  | ⑧ | the heading shares, the list-line share and marker | the mean gap of the emoji and question shares |
  - the headline facet is the one with the largest relative gap; an item unknown on either side is `Unknown` and sorts last
- Keep the facet values numeric and structured — wording belongs to the widget (T472) and the projection (T471)
- The existing `Measure` / `MeasuredProfile` stay for the current analysis until T471 replaces them

## result
- outcome: `internal/voice/fingerprint.go` counts VOICE-24's eight items with the standard library alone: `FingerprintOf(materials)` over 학습 글 (newest first for examples and ties), `MeasureText(text)` and `MeasureBlocks(blocks)` (TEXT/QUOTE prose, HEADING headings, LIST items as `-` lines, IMAGE/VIDEO skipped), each item with its threshold (`Unknown` rather than 0), numeric facets and one example sentence naming its material (none for a measured text). `Compare(voice, text)` returns one `ItemComparison` per item with both sides' facets, a headline facet and a 0…1 distance, farthest first and unknown last. `SegmentSentences` now keeps a run of end marks (`!!`, `?!`, `.~`) and the emoji or jamo right after it with the sentence it closes, so `좋아요!!` is one sentence for the counts, the readiness meter and the current analysis alike.
